## What the linked paper actually licenses as hypotheses

[G. D. Schott, *Mirror writing: neurological reflections on an unusual phenomenon*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2117809/) (2007, DOI 10.1136/jnnp.2006.094870) describes both reversed reading direction and reversed glyph forms. Crucially, its introductory section distinguishes individual reversed letters, partially reversed words, and mixtures of letter, word, and line orientation. Table 1 also mentions alternating-direction boustrophedon and reversed transfer from printing plates. It therefore does **not** justify assuming whole-page overlays as the only mechanism, nor requiring an explicit instruction naming the extraction unit before testing a bounded hypothesis. Those are investigator choices, not author rules. A literal single-glyph/partial-glyph or alternating-line check remains consistent with the clue, provided it is judged against the scan and controls. These are hypotheses, not additional clues or a solution.
